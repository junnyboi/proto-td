# Shock Trooper (`shock_trooper`) — Tier 1 Operator Specialization Design Brief

## 1. Assignment and design thesis

**Class:** Shock Trooper  
**Class ID:** `shock_trooper`  
**Promotion tier:** First tier / stage 1, promoted from `recruit`  
**Gameplay role:** Fast Vanguard  
**Operator definition:** `vanguard_1`  
**Production status:** Non-premium, recruit-derived specialization

The Shock Trooper is a practical first promotion for an ordinary adult recruit: a disciplined lane opener who reaches the front quickly, generates deployment points, and holds a narrow approach against up to two enemies. The visual thesis is **“compact breach momentum”**—a forward-set adult infantry silhouette organized around one centered, two-handed shock pike. The design should look polished, faction-literate, and promotion-worthy without acquiring the bespoke hair architecture, exposed couture, jewelry, filigree, floating mechanisms, long ornamental trains, or multi-part signature effects reserved for premium heroes.

The class resource establishes stage 1 promotion from `recruit`, the **Fast Vanguard** role, and the lane-holding deployment-point function.[1] The related operator definition uses low deployment cost, block 2, melee-only range, and a fast attack cadence, so the sprite must communicate readiness, economy of motion, and the ability to stop a narrow rush rather than heavy defensive immobility.[2]

> **Recognition sentence:** A dark, scarlet-shouldered adult line trooper braces a short gold-edged shock pike squarely through the center of the body, forming one compact forward wedge.

## 2. Reference hierarchy and closest faction language

The closest faction design language is **Crimson Aegis — the Breach Caravan**. Its blackened impact plate, scarlet shock-sails, forest-green field webbing, weapon-gold breach edges, forward wedges, ram-lances, and momentum-driven effects directly support a Fast Vanguard whose job is to enter early and contest a narrow lane.[3][4] The faction concept’s strongest applicable cues are the low forward posture, overlapping dark plate, red directional masses, gold-edged impact tools, and force concentrated along a single attack axis.[5]

This specialization deliberately uses a **reduced line-infantry interpretation** of Crimson Aegis rather than copying an ensemble hero. The large sail structures become two short mirrored shoulder fins; ornate layered plate becomes a few broad blackened segments; a hero-scale ram-lance becomes a compact service shock pike; and dramatic debris becomes a brief ground-level contact wedge. This preserves faction recognition while ensuring the unit remains materially simpler than premium heroes.

The other faction concepts were reviewed but are less appropriate. Solcrest Accord’s broad shields and upright formation authority imply a slower defensive guard; Vesper Circuit’s masks, drones, interference glass, and narrow technical asymmetry imply information warfare; Lunaris Reliquary’s rings, ritual mechanisms, and ceremonial elegance imply prestige casters or duelists.[3][6][7][8] None expresses rapid breach momentum as directly as Crimson Aegis.

## 3. Rarity and complexity ceiling

The unit must read as a well-equipped rank-and-file specialist, not a named banner character. Apply the following hierarchy:

| Design layer | Required treatment | Premium treatment to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Silhouette** | One head shape, two compact shoulder wedges, one short coat/tabard mass, one centered pike | Halos, wings, shock-sails larger than the torso, multiple orbiting parts, floor-length trains |
| **Armor** | Broad blackened chest plate, paired shoulder caps, paired bracers, paired greaves | Dense scale-by-scale rendering, sculptural pauldrons, layered jewelry, extensive engraving |
| **Textiles** | Fitted dark underlayer, short scarlet collar/shoulder fins, paired green belt tabs | Sheers, cutouts, long capes, elaborate corsetry, high-fashion asymmetry |
| **Metalwork** | Narrow gold edge on pike and a few functional fasteners | Gold filigree across every panel, chains, gems, ornamental mechanisms |
| **Energy** | One small cyan-white pike capacitor and one brief attack flash | Persistent aura, glowing eyes, orbiting energy, full-body bloom |
| **Identity** | Mature face, compact practical hair, body-type variation | Hero-specific tattoos, prosthetics, masks, named relics, elaborate coiffure |

At gameplay scale, the detail hierarchy should be approximately **70% large shapes, 25% medium construction, and 5% small accents**. The face, hand placement, weapon head, boots, and ground contact receive priority. Surface scratches, tiny buckles, and decorative seams should not be relied on for recognition.

## 4. Restrained palette and material separation

| Function | Color | Suggested value | Coverage and material |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Primary armor / outline** | Blackened charcoal | `#20242A` | Chest, paired shoulders, bracers, greaves, pike haft; matte-lacquered metal and deep textile |
| **Faction accent** | Muted breach scarlet | `#9D333A` | Short mirrored shoulder fins, inner collar, centered sternum chevron; durable woven cloth, not glossy satin |
| **Field utility** | Deep forest green | `#314A3B` | Paired belt tabs, narrow waist wrap, grip binding; coarse field webbing |
| **Weapon edge / hardware** | Aged weapon gold | `#B88943` | Pike edge, collar rivets, restrained plate edging; brushed, not jewelry-bright |
| **Energy accent** | Pale arc cyan | `#7ED6D4` | Only the pike’s central capacitor, contact seam, and one-frame impact spark |
| **Undersuit / boots** | Warm near-black | `#111316` | Fitted base layer, gloves, soles; low-reflectance textile and leather |

Hair, skin, and eye colors remain natural and may vary across recruit identities; they do not add new faction accents. Scarlet is the dominant recognition color after charcoal. Green and gold remain subordinate, and cyan occupies the least area. Do not add violet, ivory, wine-red glass, luminous gold armor planes, or multicolor effects.

## 5. Shared adult chibi silhouette

Use a **3.4-head-tall adult chibi proportion**, inside the approved three-to-four-head range.[3] The head is enlarged for readability but not toddler-rounded: retain a defined jaw, modest eye size, visible brow line, straight or slightly aquiline nose indication, and a focused closed-mouth expression. Both variants use an alert forward lean, squared shoulders, a stable split stance, and visibly developed adult hands gripping the weapon. The torso is approximately 1.05 heads, the pelvis and upper legs approximately 0.75 head, and lower legs/boots approximately 0.6 head; remaining height is distributed through neck and stance compression.

The silhouette has three reads:

1. **Top:** compact adult hair shape above two equal, outward-canted shoulder wedges;
2. **Middle:** a dark tapered armored torso crossed by no loose diagonal accessories, with both hands meeting around the centered pike shaft;
3. **Bottom:** short paired belt tabs over planted, slightly enlarged boots, creating a low forward triangle without a cape or shield.

The weapon is the tallest/narrowest shape but stays close to the body in idle. The shoulder width and boot spacing establish block-2 stability; the short hem, forward lean, and unobstructed legs establish Fast Vanguard mobility. Nothing trails beyond the back far enough to suggest a premium mantle or to create directional clipping.

## 6. Shared costume construction

Both variants wear the same issued kit and must remain recognizably matched:

- A fitted warm-black high-neck undersuit with full upper-leg coverage and practical gloves.
- A short blackened-charcoal cuirass built from one chest plane, one lower rib band, and a centered downward scarlet chevron. The chevron is a geometric color inset, not a unique heraldic emblem.
- Two identical low-profile shoulder caps, each carrying the same short scarlet triangular cloth fin. The fins point rearward equally and never exceed half a head in length.
- Two identical segmented forearm guards and two identical knee/greave plates, with only thin aged-gold functional edging.
- A deep-green waist wrap with two equal front belt tabs and two small identical utility pouches placed symmetrically behind the hips.
- Fitted dark trousers and broad-soled ankle boots. No heel distinction, exposed thigh, skirt, cape, dangling chain, medal, or side-mounted device.

The costume must preserve readable separation among matte metal, woven cloth, leather, skin, hair, and energy, consistent with the project’s shared material language.[3] All armor and equipment are serviceable, standardized, and cleanly maintained; a small amount of edge wear is acceptable, but individualized trophies are not.

## 7. Male variant

The male Shock Trooper is an **unambiguously adult man, approximately late twenties to late thirties**, with a compact athletic build, broad upper torso, sturdy neck, and mature angular face. His expression is focused and seasoned rather than angry or boyish. Use a short, brushed-back crop with a centered or near-centered hairline; the hair mass stays above the collar and has no long side fringe that changes meaning when mirrored. Light stubble may be suggested in portrait-scale art but should not become sprite noise.

His cuirass is slightly straighter through the waist and his shoulder caps sit on a broader frame. The forearms and boots may read marginally heavier, but the outer gameplay footprint, weapon length, hand positions, head height, and animation timing remain identical to the female variant. Avoid superhero bulk, exposed arms, a giant jaw, or veteran-specific trophies; he is a first-tier professional, not a premium commander.

## 8. Female variant

The female Shock Trooper is an **unambiguously adult woman, approximately late twenties to late thirties**, with a compact athletic build, strong legs, mature cheek and jaw structure, and a level, self-possessed expression. Her hair is a practical centered braided knot or compact low bun fully contained behind the head; two equal short temple sections may frame the face, but no side ponytail, long ribbon, or one-sided ornament is permitted.

Her issued armor has the same coverage, plate count, shoulder width class, belt geometry, boots, gloves, and centered chevron as the male kit. The cuirass may taper modestly at the waist and the face and hip construction may differentiate the adult body type, but do not add a breast-window, exposed midriff, skirt, high heel, jewelry, eyelashes exaggerated beyond sprite readability, or a narrower weapon. She must project adult frontline competence and share the same gameplay footprint and equipment authority as the male variant.

## 9. Weapon — service shock pike

The sole weapon is a **two-handed service shock pike**, a compact Crimson Aegis-derived ram-lance approximately **1.25 times the operator’s body height**. It uses a dark hexagonal haft, two equal grip collars, a short centered spear point with bilaterally matched gold-edged impact flanges, a small cyan capacitor exactly on the weapon centerline, and a weighted symmetrical butt cap. Its profile should read as a narrow spear crossed with a compact breaching ram, not a rifle, banner, drill, halberd, or premium relic.

In idle, both hands hold the shaft in front of the sternum: rear hand near the lower ribcage, lead hand near the waist, with the weapon angled only along the selected facing axis. At anticipation, the hands slide apart symmetrically along the same shaft. No hand swaps, detached pieces, telescoping side assembly, muzzle, shield, or off-hand focus are used. The centered capacitor provides the only persistent energy point and remains small enough that the gold spearhead—not glow—defines the weapon.

The pike is appropriate to the class because its direct line establishes a narrow-lane blocker, its compact length supports fast deployment and quick cadence, and its ram-derived head connects clearly to Crimson Aegis momentum language without inheriting hero-scale ornament.

## 10. Idle animation

Follow the current operator convention of four logical diagonal directions (`ne`, `nw`, `se`, `sw`) with a **24-frame idle strip at 12 fps**, yielding a restrained two-second loop.[9] Author each frame in a 192 × 192 cell, preserve the complete centered subject, and target the established 58 px runtime display height and 106 px normalized subject height.[9] The relevant `vanguard_1` presentation currently specifies a centered horizontal pivot and low foot anchor; final exports should preserve that runtime ground contact rather than visually drifting between directions.[9]

The idle is a subtle **brace-and-breathe loop**:

- **Frames 1–6:** neutral low-ready stance; shoulders settle downward by no more than 1–2 source pixels, elbows soften, and the pike capacitor is dim.
- **Frames 7–12:** chest rises slightly; both hands and the centered pike lift together by roughly 1 source pixel. Weight remains distributed across both feet.
- **Frames 13–18:** the cyan capacitor brightens gently and a narrow light seam travels once toward the spearhead. The light remains inside the weapon silhouette.
- **Frames 19–24:** chest and hands settle to the starting pose; the light fades and the paired shoulder fins return with only a one-pixel secondary follow-through.

There is **no stepping, hopping, lateral hip sway, weapon flourish, cloth snapping, head bobbing, or free-running electricity**. Boots remain planted and the root/pivot does not move. The operator may make one tiny forward-facing eye/brow reset over the loop, but blinking is optional at gameplay scale. The first and last silhouettes must match cleanly.

## 11. In-place attack and recovery

Follow the current convention of a **13-frame attack strip at 12 fps** for each logical diagonal direction.[9] The attack is a concise **brace-thrust-discharge-recover** cycle; it must read from anticipation, weapon-line extension, one contact flash, and a complete return to the idle guard. The operator attacks in place: the center of the pelvis stays over the placement tile, the rear boot remains locked, and the forward boot may slide visually no more than 2–3 source pixels without moving the runtime node or hitbox.

| Frames | Phase | Required action and read |
|---:|---|---|
| **1–3** | Anticipation | Hips and shoulders compress directly backward along the facing axis; elbows draw in; pike pulls to the sternum; capacitor rises from dim cyan to pale cyan. Silhouette becomes shorter and denser. |
| **4–5** | Drive | Operator extends both arms and torso forward, guiding the pike straight through the body centerline. The rear leg stays braced and both shoulder fins trail equally. |
| **6** | Contact | Maximum reach. A compact, bilateral wedge-shaped cyan-white flash forms around the spearhead; a thin gold impact rim may appear at the target-side edge. No body translation, screen shake baked into art, or effect behind the feet. |
| **7–8** | Recoil | Pike rebounds a short distance; elbows bend; torso rocks back without crossing the idle rear boundary. One tiny symmetrical spark pair collapses into the capacitor. |
| **9–11** | Re-seat | Weapon returns to the centered low-ready line, front boot re-plants, shoulder fins settle, and glow quickly drops below contact intensity. |
| **12–13** | Recovery | Full idle silhouette and hand positions are restored. Hold enough visual stability for the next cadence to begin without a pop. |

The contact effect occupies a narrow cone no wider than the operator’s shoulders and extends only toward the attacked cell. It must not imply area damage, ranged fire, stun chains, or an oversized hitbox not supported by gameplay. The pike tip is the single contact point in every direction. There is no spin, side slash, leap, charge across the tile, or persistent afterimage.

## 12. Horizontal mirroring and directional safety contract

The design is explicitly **horizontal-mirror-safe**. Horizontal mirroring may exchange left and right pixels, but it must never change equipment meaning, combat handedness, emblem identity, or collision expectations.

1. The pike, capacitor, point, butt cap, and both hand grips are centered or bilaterally symmetrical. It is always used with two hands and has no designated powered side.
2. Shoulder caps, scarlet fins, bracers, gloves, greaves, boot armor, belt tabs, and pouches occur as identical pairs.
3. The only chest marking is a centered abstract chevron. Do not add faction text, letters, numbers, directional arrows, rank stripes, heraldry, or a one-sided emblem.
4. No shield, prosthetic, sidearm, radio, cable, scabbard, badge, eye patch, monocle, mask half, hip battery, one-sided holster, or single shoulder mechanism may be introduced.
5. Hair remains compact and functionally symmetrical in mass. Minor natural strand variation is acceptable only if it carries no semantic or equipment information.
6. The attack line, contact wedge, sparks, and cyan seam are symmetric around the facing axis. Do not bake a left-to-right swing or a one-sided projectile origin into mirrored frames.
7. Root, foot anchor, selection footprint, and hitbox remain centered and unchanged through idle, attack, recovery, and mirrored playback. Weapon reach is visual only and always points into the selected target direction.
8. If paired directional art is generated independently rather than mirrored at runtime, the same rules still apply: `ne`/`nw` and `se`/`sw` must preserve identical apparent body scale, weapon reach, foot contact, palette placement, and timing.

## 13. Sprite production contract and review gates

Current recruit and `vanguard_1` animated assets use 192 × 192 frames assembled as 24-frame idle strips (4608 × 192) and 13-frame attack strips (2496 × 192), across four diagonal directions.[9][10] Preserve this structure unless the runtime contract is deliberately revised. Camera, projection, subject scale, frame origin, lighting direction, and ground contact must be locked across all strips. Keep the complete head, boots, pike point, butt cap, and contact effect inside every frame.

Male and female variants should be reviewed as a pair using neutral, anticipation, contact, recoil, and recovered frames at native gameplay scale. Approval requires all of the following:

| Gate | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| **Adult read** | Both faces, posture, hands, costume, and body construction clearly communicate adults aged 21+; neither resembles a school-age or toddler-proportioned character. |
| **Role read** | At a glance, the forward wedge, planted split stance, short hem, and narrow pike communicate fast melee lane control rather than defender, sniper, or caster behavior. |
| **Faction read** | Blackened plate, muted scarlet wedge accents, green webbing, and gold-edged ram-pike evoke Crimson Aegis without hero-scale sails or debris. |
| **Tier read** | Finish is polished, but shapes are standardized and economical; no ornate relic, bespoke asymmetric couture, jewelry, halo, drone, elaborate hair, or persistent aura is present. |
| **Variant parity** | Male and female have the same equipment, coverage, color placement, weapon authority, frame footprint, reach, timing, and effects; anatomy and mature face/hair construction provide the differentiation. |
| **Silhouette read** | At 58 px display height, the two shoulder wedges, centered pike, short waist tabs, and planted boots remain distinguishable without interior linework. |
| **Motion read** | Idle is quiet and seamless; attack has clear anticipation, one-frame contact, recoil, and recovery while remaining rooted in place. |
| **Mirror safety** | A horizontal flip introduces no semantic swap, hand-dominance change, one-sided device, emblem reversal, effect-origin error, or hitbox discrepancy. |

## 14. Explicit exclusions

Do not add a shield, banner, firearm, oversized drill, second weapon, side-mounted battery, floating focus, cape, long shock-sail, asymmetric pauldron, exposed mechanical limb, one-sided visor, large faction seal, written insignia, trophy, skirt, high heel, cutout, jewelry set, loose waist chain, glowing eyes, orbiting sparks, or full-body electrical aura. Do not differentiate the variants by making the woman decorative and the man armored. Do not animate locomotion inside the attack. Do not let the pike or effects exceed the authored frame, change length by direction, or imply attacks outside the melee target cell.
