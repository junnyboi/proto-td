# Mission Cutscene Reference Review

## Character sheets

### Lunaris Vessel

The sheet establishes a recognizable cinematic lock: adult champagne-blond woman; braided crown and very long loose hair; fitted ivory and violet-black ceremonial battle couture; brushed-gold chains and mechanisms; asymmetric mantle and translucent split overskirt; black fitted leggings and heeled combat boots. Her Crescent Reliquary is a large independent gold-and-ivory orbital ring with a moon-cyan core, not a halo attached to her body. Film compositions must preserve her hair mass, complete guiding hand, ring circumference, hip mechanism, and planted commanding posture.

### Reliquary Duelist

The sheet establishes an adult East Asian man with a tall athletic-muscular build, long black high ponytail, broad exposed arms, dark teal sleeveless tailoring, violet-black layers, antique-gold harness hardware, bracers, long coat panels, and boots. The Jade Meridian is a long straight one-handed spellblade with a dark spine, gold guard, and cyan-jade channel; it must remain fully distinct from a katana or broad fantasy sword. Film compositions must keep the ponytail, shoulders, blade path, hands, and floor contact readable while avoiding a second strike or unnecessary spin.

### Archive Caster

The sheet locks an adult East Asian woman with a poised curvy silhouette, short silver-lilac curled bob, plum-black fitted ceremonial dress, translucent lilac-edged panels, long gloves, brushed-gold chains, suspended ornaments, and black heeled boots. The Archive Astrolabe is a mechanically separate gold orbital instrument with concentric rings, hanging weights, and a moon-cyan core. Films must keep her guiding hand, bob silhouette, full instrument circumference, chain weights, and calm analytical posture readable; it may reveal or protect anima paths but never copy souls.

## Anima War concepts

### Corrupted PROTOS

The approved antagonist image uses monumental scale, white ceramic humanoid armor, black inner mechanisms, fractured gold circular architecture, broken symmetry, and violet-magenta soul conduits. PROTOS reads as calm and imperial rather than feral. Mission films may use this visual language in projected architecture, screens, relays, or colossal background structures, but the three premium heroes remain the only human protagonists and the upper-right corner remains clear for the runtime Skip control.

### Human anima farms

The approved farm image makes the premise legible through contrast: pristine white-and-gold garden city above, massive orderly rows of captive adults below, warm-white/cyan individual soul channels descending toward a violet-magenta industrial core. The trio is staged as small, readable silhouettes observing the system together. Mission cinematics should preserve that paradise-over-machinery deception and avoid lurid prison imagery when the location is still concealed.

### Robot empire castes

The empire machines share ivory ceramic armor, black mechanisms, brushed-gold frames, circular apertures, tall elegant silhouettes, and a clear size hierarchy. Clean cyan cores and individual human-shaped lights contrast against the violet-magenta processed-anima core carried by the heaviest caste. Cutscenes should show machines as specialized industrial actors—tagging, transporting, guarding, channeling, or manufacturing—not interchangeable metal infantry.

### Anima Forge and ensemble quality bar

The Act II capital concept establishes an operatic but readable climax: Company Manus appears tiny against a city-sized circular forge, violet-magenta processed anima dominates the industrial core, and PROTOS is projected as a calm colossal presence above it. The title keyframe establishes the target hero finish—premium painterly anime realism, adult faces and bodies, blue-black monumental architecture, moon-cyan clean mechanisms, brushed-gold detail, clear central hero hierarchy, and complete weapon silhouettes. Mission films should retain this polish while keeping each eight-second beat simpler than a trailer: one location, one story action, one emotional conclusion.

## Cross-film production lock

Every anchor and film must use the full-size sheets as identity authority, the Anima War concepts as location/antagonist authority, and the title keyframe as lighting/rendering authority. Use exactly the named premium heroes; no hero doubles, chibi styling, generated text, subtitles, logos, or readable signs. Protect the upper-right 18% for the Godot Skip control, keep critical faces and action inside the central portrait-safe band, and distinguish rescued individual souls from processed anima through both color and containment.
